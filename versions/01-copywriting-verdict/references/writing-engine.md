# Writing Engine

Use this sequence for generation.

```text
context -> audience state -> visible facts -> text contribution -> battlefield -> user desire -> concrete nail -> main attack angle -> line experiments -> meltdown checks -> final pick
```

## Object Sense Gate

Write the line as speech addressed to a particular person in a particular moment. Know what that person is already looking at, what they care about, and what thought the copy should cause next.

For visual-led posts, the copy must do at least one job the visual cannot do alone.

- Point attention toward a consequential detail
- Interpret how a visible choice changes the scene
- Extend the scene into a credible human or commercial consequence
- Invite a specific judgment, comparison, or next action

Do not use the caption as visual transcription. `The video shows`, `this post presents`, `a closer look at`, `the footage captures`, and inventory such as `front, surface, rear structure` are kill signals when they merely report what the viewer can see. Keep them only when the genre is explicitly a report, accessibility description, inspection record, or technical walkthrough.

For displays, environments, products, and spatial visuals, prefer this reasoning chain.

```text
visible design choice -> perceptual or practical change -> value in the viewer's situation
```

Example shape:

```text
two illuminated walls meet at a corner
-> the artwork continues into a second sightline
-> the booth can address people approaching from more than one direction
```

Do not publish the chain as a report. Compress its most useful turn into natural speech.

## Direction Generation

Generate at least three distinct directions.

Each direction must use a different attack angle.

Useful direction types:

- Kill the old solution
- Shift the reference frame
- Make the buyer feel worthy
- Turn a feature into a concrete scene
- Turn a hidden desire into permission
- Make the sound carry the meaning
- Pair a hook with proof
- Use recursion when the product has a self-referential property

## Candidate Discipline

Do not keep lines just because they are fluent.

Kill lines that are interchangeable, abstract, self-centered, proofless, or only clever.

Kill lines that remain true but add nothing to the audience's experience of the visual.

For each final recommendation, explain why it survives.

## Line Experiment Patterns

Use these as experiment shapes, not as formulas.

### Exclusionary Verdict

Declare the standard solution invalid.

Pattern:

```text
No [standard fix]. Just [deeper mechanism].
```

### Reference Frame Shift

Move the comparison from local competitors to a larger time, world, identity, or category frame.

Pattern:

```text
Not the best in [category]. The line that defines [larger frame].
```

### Identity Altar

Turn purchase into self-confirmation.

Pattern:

```text
Because [user identity or value] deserves [product outcome].
```

### Sensory Nail

Replace abstract benefit with a visible, audible, or touchable object.

Pattern:

```text
[Concrete object, place, number, sound, or gesture] proves [benefit].
```

### Desire Permission

Name a hidden desire and make it acceptable.

Pattern:

```text
Yes, you wanted [desire]. Good.
```

### Indirect Praise

Let a third party, object, rival, or scene praise the product.

Pattern:

```text
Your [third party] might [emotion].
```

### Idiom Hack

Borrow a known phrase, then break the expected ending.

Pattern:

```text
[Known phrase setup] ... [brand-specific reversal].
```

### Acoustic Hook

Use sound, rhythm, repetition, pop, friction, or rupture to make memory easier.

Pattern:

```text
[Sound or rhythm] becomes the benefit.
```

### Meta-Loop

Use recursion when the product acts on its own tool, process, or category.

Pattern:

```text
Make the [tool/process] [do its own promised action].
```

## Hook-Proof-Action Pattern

When the medium allows more than one line, use this structure.

Hook line  
Creates attention, tension, identity, or reframe.

Proof line  
Gives concrete reason, evidence, scene, number, mechanism, or contrast.

Action line  
Pushes the user toward the next step without breaking the tone.

## Spoken Voice Test

Read the final line aloud as if showing the image or video to one real buyer, creator, or colleague.

- If it sounds like a report, product database, museum label, or content-management note, rewrite it.
- If no real person would say it in that moment, rewrite the thought rather than swapping adjectives.
- If removing the line costs the viewer no meaning, interest, judgment, or action, delete it.
